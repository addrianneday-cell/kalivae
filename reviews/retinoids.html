Retinol, retinal and tretinoin: what really works for photoaging?

The skincare aisle can make every vitamin A product sound identical. They aren’t.

Published 5 November 2026 · 6 min read · Editorial review, not individually medically reviewed
Evidence rating Strong What this means How we grade →
By Kalivae editorial Skin

The short version: Topical tretinoin has good evidence for improving some signs of sun-related aging; results from cosmetic retinol products are less uniform.

Same family, different formulas

Retinoids are vitamin A derivatives used in skincare. Prescription tretinoin is a specific drug with decades of clinical research; over-the-counter retinol and retinaldehyde vary by concentration, formulation and conversion in skin. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

What is well supported

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled eight randomized trials involving 1,361 participants and found that topical tretinoin improved both fine and coarse wrinkles associated with photodamage compared with inactive vehicle products. Other reviews also support improvements in certain uneven pigmentation and texture outcomes.

What results look like in real life

Think months of consistent use, not an overnight filter. Irritation, dryness and peeling are common hurdles, especially when starting too aggressively. Gentle cleansing, moisturizer and sun protection are part of the plan, not optional extras.

Where retinol fits

Cosmetic retinol may help appearance for some users and can be more approachable, but products differ and cannot automatically inherit every result from prescription tretinoin trials. Compare formula, tolerability, packaging and your own skin goals instead of chasing the highest advertised percentage.

One non-negotiable caveat

Retinoid use should be reviewed with a healthcare professional if you are pregnant, trying to become pregnant or breastfeeding; topical retinoids are generally avoided during pregnancy. Use broad-spectrum sunscreen and consult a dermatologist for persistent irritation or specific skin conditions.

The bottom line

Prescription tretinoin has the strongest evidence for photoaging; over-the-counter retinol is in the same family with less consistent data. Either way, the slow stuff matters most: starting low, using it at night, and wearing sunscreen the next morning. Those unglamorous habits are a bigger part of the result than the exact bottle on the shelf.

Research and further reading

  2. Sitohang et al., 2022 review of randomized trials
