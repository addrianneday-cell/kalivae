Massage guns: do they actually help recovery?

A massage gun can feel great after a hard day. That doesn’t necessarily mean it is rebuilding your muscles faster.

Published 5 November 2026 · 6 min read · Editorial review, not individually medically reviewed
Evidence rating Moderate What this means How we grade →
By Kalivae editorial Movement & Recovery

The short version: Percussive devices may temporarily improve flexibility or range of motion; recovery and performance claims are mixed.

What people love about them

Percussive massage devices deliver repeated pulses into soft tissue. Many users like the sensation of loosening tight muscles, especially after workouts or long hours sitting. Enjoyment is real, but research has to evaluate specific outcomes.

What the studies say

A 2023 systematic review of 11 studies found some evidence for short-term flexibility and range-of-motion benefits. It did not consistently support strength, balance, speed or explosive-performance improvements. A later review of randomized trials found uncertain results for soreness and maximal strength recovery, even when some performance markers improved.

So are they worth it?

They can be a reasonable comfort tool if you like using one, especially when you keep expectations modest. They are not a substitute for sleep, sensible training loads, rehabilitation or professional evaluation of an injury.

How to use one thoughtfully

Start gently and follow device instructions. Avoid applying force directly over bones, the front of the neck, open wounds or an acutely injured area. People with clotting concerns, altered sensation or specific medical conditions should seek medical advice first.

Buying priorities

Comfort, adjustable intensity, weight, battery reliability and customer support may matter more to daily experience than bold recovery slogans. If you cannot tell what clinical outcome a brand means by “recovery,” ask before you buy.

The bottom line

Massage guns have modest support for short-term range of motion and feel pleasant on sore muscles. The bigger claims about recovery and performance are not as settled. If you enjoy using one, treat it as a comfort tool rather than therapy, keep it off bony areas and injuries, and spend the bulk of your recovery budget on sleep, hydration and protein.
